AI and the Case of La Défense
Philippe Morel, Julian Besems, Soungmin Yu

In 2000, when speculating about the architecture and urban planning of the future, it seemed to some of us that the future of the city would be post-urban. The built environment would no longer be made up of entities identifiable as architectures, but of entities simply defined by technical specifications imposed by computational and logistics economics. These entities were reduced to only three types relevant to a global production system: The vertical box (the skyscraper), the horizontal box (the shed) and the individual cubic box (the house).




Today, if we look at the skyscraper, we cannot help thinking that with the advent of computationalism it will undergo a major change. The replacement of intellectual tasks by extraordinary Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools forces us to question the very existence of skyscrapers and central business districts (CBDs) whose economic rationality is no longer evident. Through a study of CBDs around the world and a case study of Europe’s largest one (Paris La Défense) our research cluster and its students speculate about AI’s impacts. By making extensive use of data from various sources and AI tools (including deep learning technics), students do not solely speculate on the future of a specific CBD, they design it.
