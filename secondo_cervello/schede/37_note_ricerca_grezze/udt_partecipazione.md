## Core definitions and critiques of UDT in planning

### Takeaway
There is no agreed definition of "digital twin" for cities. Reviews describe it as 3D city model plus semantics, sensors and simulation. A consistent critique, in 2022 to 2026 papers, is that UDTs are technocentric, rely on few variables, and participation is constrained by design.

### Cited Findings
- Ketzler et al. 2020, "Digital Twins for Cities: A State of the Art Review", Built Environment 46(4), 547-573, DOI 10.2148/benv.46.4.547. They find no agreed definition; the term is used for systems that go beyond 3D city models by adding semantic data, real-time sensor data, physical models and simulations; grey literature is used to examine implementations. [opened] — [Chalmers record](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/520594)
- Batty 2024, "Digital twins in city planning", Nature Computational Science 4, 192-199, DOI 10.1038/s43588-024-00606-7. Perspective surveying models from aggregate economic/behavioural approaches to agent-based, cellular and micro-simulation, and the computational difficulty of geospatial urban work. [opened, abstract only] — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10190386)
- Dembski, Wössner, Letzgus, Ruddat, Yamu 2020, "Urban Digital Twins for Smart Cities and Citizens: The Case Study of Herrenberg, Germany", Sustainability 12, 2307. Prototype combining 3D model, space syntax, traffic and wind simulation and volunteered geographic information, shown in VR at participation events; survey of 39 participants; authors admit small non-representative sample, no test over a full planning process, and that the twin abstracts away much social, economic and environmental data. [opened] — [PDF](https://living-in.eu/sites/default/files/files/sustainability-12-02307-v2.pdf)
- Caprari, Castelli, Montuori, Camardelli, Malvezzi 2022, "Digital Twin for Urban Planning in the Green Deal Era: A State of the Art and Future Perspectives", Sustainability 14(10). Compares six case studies; warns that public-private investment in smart/twin cities could push data-driven planning toward unrealistic expectations; DTs are "largely experimental in practice". [opened, via abstract summary] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v14y2022i10p6263-d820381.html)
- Schrotter and Hürzeler 2020, "The Digital Twin of the City of Zurich for Urban Planning", PFG 88, 99-112. Authors (City of Zurich staff) say planning tools are often static and involve few citizens; report 3D uses in the municipal plan, high-rise planning, urban climate, competitions, and a participation tool still being evaluated. Self-reported; several uses are pilots. [opened] — [PDF](https://living-in.eu/sites/default/files/files/the-digital-twin-of-the-city-of-zurich-for-urban-planning.pdf)
- "Urban scale digital twins in data-driven society: Challenging digital universalism in urban planning decision-making", 2022, SAGE, vol. 20(2) (journal name, authors and DOI not shown on the record). Argues that although DTs aim to increase citizen participation, reliance on a limited set of variables and processes is problematic; traces the smart-city shift from technical to socio-technical perspective. [opened, partial metadata] — [ETH Research Collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/71b356d7-6d34-40a6-a039-e01d0793fbd0)
- De Jaeger and Swerts 2026, "Right to the digital Twin City? citizen participation and limits-by-design in Rotterdam's urban digital twin", Cities 168, 106498, DOI 10.1016/j.cities.2025.106498. Frames analysis as the "Right to the Digital Twin City"; finds participation constrained by "limits-by-design" (planners controlled design, no link to wider municipal decision-making, limited communication, digital divide, language barriers, distrust). The record does not mention Lefebvre. [opened] — [Erasmus Pure](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/right-to-the-digital-twin-city-citizen-participation-and-limits-b/)
- Altındaş, Özçam, Dane (TU/e), Velibeyoğlu 2026, "A Participatory Urban Digital Twin Development Roadmap for Climate-Responsive Urban Planning: The Case of İzmir, Türkiye", Urban Planning 11, DOI 10.17645/up.12635. Criticises "technocentric" UDTs "developed with minimal stakeholder engagement"; proposes a three-stage workshop process and governance model. [opened] — [Cogitatio](https://www.cogitatiopress.com/urbanplanning/article/view/12635)
- Delsante et al. 2025, "Participatory design through the Digital twin city tool: Actualization of the concept and critical perspectives", Rivista di Digital Politics V(1), 3-24, DOI 10.53227/117419; keywords include Citizen Participation and Social Justice. Abstract not retrievable. [opened, metadata only] — [Rivisteweb](https://rivisteweb.it/doi/10.53227/117419)

### Inferences
- The gap between technical UDT and planning practice appears in three forms across these sources: definitional vagueness (Ketzler), experimental status and hype (Caprari), and participation limited by design and governance (De Jaeger and Swerts; Altındaş et al.). Azadi's "sociotechnical integration" framing fits this pattern, but this is my inference.
- Dembski et al. and Schrotter/Hürzeler are constructive practice examples, useful as the "what UDTs do" baseline against which critiques are read.

## Knowledge graphs / semantic web / ontologies for city models

### Takeaway
Only one 2020-2026 source was verified: CityGML 3.0 has been converted to an OWL ontology network ("CityOWL"). Nothing was verified on stakeholder/value ontologies or SOLID.

### Cited Findings
- Vinasco-Alvarez, Samuel, Servigne, Gesquière 2024, "Towards an Automated Transformation of an nD Urban Data Model to a Computational Ontology Network: From UML to OWL, From CityGML 3.0 to 'CityOWL'", ISPRS Annals X-4/W4-2024, 231-238, DOI 10.5194/isprs-annals-X-4-W4-2024-231-2024. Proposes standards-based UML-to-OWL conversion and alignments with GeoSPARQL, SKOS, OWL-Time. [opened] — [ISPRS Annals](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-4-W4-2024/231/2024/)
- Ketzler et al. 2020 list semantic data as one ingredient distinguishing UDTs from 3D city models (see above). [opened]

### Inferences
- A planning-oriented knowledge graph would plausibly need to link city-model ontologies like CityOWL with ontologies of actors, values and decisions; no verified source for the latter was found.

## Relevance to housing and participatory social housing design

### Takeaway
Little direct evidence was verified. One conference abstract shows a need for resident-facing digital co-design tools for housing.

### Cited Findings
- Raposo and Eloy 2022, "Using digital tools to facilitate co-design in housing projects", 27th IAPS Conference book of abstracts, Iscte Lisbon. States existing housing customisation tools were built without input from potential users; aims to define requirements for a digital tool letting inhabitants take part in designing their homes. [opened] — [Ciência-IUL](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/using-digital-tools-to-facilitate-co-design-in-housing-projects/89628)
