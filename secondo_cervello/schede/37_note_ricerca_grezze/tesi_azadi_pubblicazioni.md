## 2. What do the sources say about the framework, FMU, knowledge graph, workshops?

### Takeaway
Only abstract-level content could be verified. The thesis abstract defines AUP's three dimensions; the CUPUM abstract gives the only concrete workshop facts (12 expert stakeholders, 3 sessions, Eindhoven centre densification, 12 stakeholder-specific UDTs).

### Cited Findings
- Thesis abstract: gap between technical ambitions of Urban Digital Twins and their value for the social, value-laden nature of planning. AUP has three dimensions: Interdisciplinary Integration (bridging silos, shared language), Consensual Contextualization (representing diverse stakeholder views to support consensus), Procedural Operationalization (embedding representations in decision-making processes). Also presents the FMU Ontology, a participatory UDTing approach, a workshop series, an open-source toolbox and a graph-based evaluation framework using a Dynamic Knowledge Graph; UDTs reframed as evolving collections of urban data and models, not static mirrors of reality. — [Pure](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/augmented-urban-planning-a-systematic-framework-for-sociotechnica/)
- FMU paper (P2): UDTs often fail to link disciplines, reflect diverse stakeholder views, or adapt to changing planning processes; FMU Ontology aims at (a) interoperability across disciplinary data and models, (b) networks of stakeholder-specific digital twins for engagement/consensus, (c) embedding twins in planning workflows so they evolve with stakeholder priorities. Validated by consistency and competency tests; case study: strategic urban densification in Eindhoven. Keywords include knowledge graph. — [Pure](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/formalizing-modelling-utilizing-ontology-a-semantic-framework-for/), [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/saeenvirb/v_3a53_3ay_3a2026_3ai_3a4_3ap_3a804-830.htm)
- Workshops (P3): digitally interactive workshops with 12 expert stakeholders on densifying Eindhoven's city centre; three sessions; procedural data, discussions and questionnaires collected; result = 12 stakeholder-specific UDTs each with planning scenarios explored/evaluated; assessed against the three perspectives II, CC, PO; authors "report progress, identify areas for further research". Funding: Urban Development Initiative's Urban Planning Re-imagined (UDI-URP) and Regional Deal Brainport Eindhoven. — [Pure](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/stakeholder-specific-urban-digital-twins-as-means-for-participato/)
- Review (P1): 88 articles; UDT ambitions (first discussed 2018) largely unrealized because of socio-technical challenges. — [Pure](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/what-have-urban-digital-twins-contributed-to-urban-planning-and-d/)

### Inferences
- "Entropy" among the fingerprint topics is an automated tag; it may hint that the DKG evaluation uses information-theoretic measures, but this is speculation.
- Wording "dynamic knowledge graph" echoes the Cambridge/CMCL "Universal Digital Twin – A Dynamic Knowledge Graph" line (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/data-centric-engineering/article/universal-digital-twin-a-dynamic-knowledge-graph/FD25CDFF886CD2ED33D1FDFC13F6BEAB, search result only, not opened); whether Azadi builds on it is unknown.
